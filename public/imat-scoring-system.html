IMAT Scoring System 2027: How Points, Cutoffs & Rankings Work

By ItaloStudy Editorial Team — Italy admissions & entrance exam specialists  |  Last updated: November 2026  |  About us

Master the IMAT scoring system to maximize your score. Learn when to guess, when to skip, and what score you need for your target university.

How IMAT Scoring Works

IMAT uses a straightforward but strategic scoring system. Understanding it is crucial — the difference between guessing and skipping a question can determine whether you get into your dream university.

+1.5
Correct Answer
-0.4
Wrong Answer
0
Unanswered
Score = (Correct × 1.5) + (Wrong × -0.4) + (Blank × 0)
Maximum possible score: 90 points (60 correct) | Minimum: -24 points (60 wrong)
Example: A student answers 40 questions correctly, 12 incorrectly, and leaves 8 blank:
Score = (40 × 1.5) + (12 × -0.4) + (8 × 0)
Score = 60 + (-4.8) + 0
Score = 55.2 points — This is an excellent score, competitive for any university.

💡 Important: Cutoffs Change Every Year

Cutoff scores fluctuate based on exam difficulty and number of applicants. A score of 40 might get you into Sapienza one year but not another. Always aim higher than the minimum — target at least 5-10 points above last year's cutoff for safety.

The Guessing Strategy: When to Guess vs. Skip

This is where understanding the math behind IMAT scoring gives you a real advantage. Let's break down the expected value of guessing:

Scenario 1: Random Guess (Can't Eliminate Any Options)

With 5 options, each has a 1/5 (20%) chance of being correct.

Expected Value = (0.20 × 1.5) + (0.80 × -0.4)
= 0.30 + (-0.32)
= -0.02 per question

Verdict: Slightly negative — skip these questions

Scenario 2: Eliminate 1 Option (4 Options Left)

With 4 options, each has a 1/4 (25%) chance of being correct.

Expected Value = (0.25 × 1.5) + (0.75 × -0.4)
= 0.375 + (-0.30)
= +0.075 per question

Verdict: Slightly positive — guess is marginally better than skipping

Scenario 3: Eliminate 2 Options (3 Options Left)

With 3 options, each has a 1/3 (33%) chance of being correct.

Expected Value = (0.33 × 1.5) + (0.67 × -0.4)
= 0.50 + (-0.27)
= +0.23 per question

Verdict: Clearly positive — always guess when you can eliminate 2 options

Scenario 4: Eliminate 3 Options (2 Options Left — 50/50)

Expected Value = (0.50 × 1.5) + (0.50 × -0.4)
= 0.75 + (-0.20)
= +0.55 per question

Verdict: Strongly positive — always guess on 50/50
🎯 The Rule of Thumb: If you can eliminate at least 1 option with confidence, guess. If you truly have zero idea (all 5 options look equally likely), skip. Over a 60-question exam, this strategy can add 3-5 points to your score compared to either always guessing or never guessing.

How University Rankings and Reallocations Work

After the exam, here's what happens:

  1. Score Publication: Results are published on the CINECA portal within 2-3 weeks of the exam
  2. First Ranking: Students are ranked by score for each university they selected during registration. You can typically choose up to 3 university preferences.
  3. First Allocation: Top-ranked students are offered places at their first-choice university. You have 3-5 days to accept or decline.
  4. Reallocation Rounds (2-4 rounds): As students decline offers (because they got into a higher-choice university or decided not to attend), seats free up and are offered to the next students in the ranking.
  5. Final Enrollment: Once you accept a place, you have a deadline to complete enrollment and pay the first tuition installment.
⚠️ Critical: If you're offered a place at your 2nd or 3rd choice university, accepting it removes you from the ranking for your 1st choice. Think carefully before accepting — if you're close to the cutoff for your 1st choice, it might be worth waiting for reallocation. But there's risk: reallocation is not guaranteed.

Score Optimization: Section-by-Section Strategy

Section 1: Logic & GK (22 Questions = 33 Points Max)

Target: 14-16 correct, 4-6 wrong, 2-4 skipped = 19-22 points

  • Logical reasoning (10 Qs): These are trainable — aim for 7-8 correct
  • General knowledge (12 Qs): High variance — aim for 6-8 correct, skip confidently unknown ones

Section 2: Biology (18 Questions = 27 Points Max)

Target: 13-15 correct, 2-3 wrong, 1-2 skipped = 19-22 points

This is the highest-value section. Deep study pays off most here.

Section 3: Chemistry (12 Questions = 18 Points Max)

Target: 8-10 correct, 1-2 wrong, 1-2 skipped = 11-14 points

Section 4: Physics & Math (8 Questions = 12 Points Max)

Target: 5-7 correct, 0-1 wrong, 1-2 skipped = 7-10 points

Math questions (3) are the quickest wins — formulas are straightforward.

🎯 Score Target Summary

Following this section strategy gives you approximately 56-68 points, which is competitive for any Italian university. The key is maximizing Biology and Logical Reasoning — these 40 questions account for 60 of the 90 possible points.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is IMAT scored?
+1.5 for correct, -0.4 for wrong, 0 for blank. Maximum 90 points, minimum -24. The scoring penalizes random guessing but rewards educated guessing — if you can eliminate 1+ options, it's statistically better to guess.
What's a good IMAT score?
45+ is competitive for top universities like Sapienza and Bologna. 35-44 is good for mid-tier universities. 55+ is excellent and virtually guarantees admission anywhere. Below 25 is unlikely to secure a seat.
Should I guess on IMAT?
If you can eliminate at least 1 option: yes, guess. The expected value turns positive once you're choosing from 4 options instead of 5. If you truly have no clue about all 5 options, skip the question.
How do university rankings work?
Students are ranked by score for each university they selected. Places are offered in multiple rounds (typically 3-4 reallocations). If top-ranked students decline, their seats go to the next students. Many students get admitted during reallocation rounds.
Is there negative marking in IMAT?
Yes, -0.4 points per wrong answer. However, the penalty is lenient compared to exams like NEET (-1 per wrong). The math shows that even eliminating 1 out of 5 options makes guessing slightly profitable in expected value.
