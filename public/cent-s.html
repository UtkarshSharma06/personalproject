CEnT-S 2026: Your Complete Guide to Italy's STEM Entrance Exam

Everything you need to know about the CISIA English Test for Science — how it works, what is on the syllabus, how scoring and normalization affect your ranking, and how to register. Written by the ItaloStudy editorial team.

Last Updated May 01, 2026
Reading Time 18 Minutes
Status Verified Official

01. What is the CEnT-S?

The CEnT-S (CISIA English Test – Science) is the entrance exam you need to take if you want to study Engineering, Computer Science, Physics, Mathematics, or certain Economics programs taught in English at Italian public universities.

It replaced the older English TOLC-I format in late 2024, bringing a more standardized system. The test is organized by CISIA (Consorzio Interuniversitario Sistemi Integrati per l'Accesso), the national body that manages university entrance testing across Italy.

Note for 2026 applicants: Your CEnT-S score is valid only for the 2026/2027 academic year. If you took a test in early 2025, check with your target university's admission call (bando) to confirm whether it is still accepted.

02. How the Test is Structured

The CEnT-S is computer-based and taken through the CISIA platform. You will face 55 multiple-choice questions across five sections, with a total time limit of 110 minutes. Each section has its own timer, and once you move on to the next section, you cannot go back.

Subject Section Questions Time Allocated
Mathematics 15 Questions 30 Minutes
Reasoning on Texts & Data 15 Questions 30 Minutes
Biology 10 Questions 20 Minutes
Chemistry 10 Questions 20 Minutes
Physics 5 Questions 10 Minutes
Total 55 Questions 110 Minutes

03. Detailed Syllabus Breakdown

The CEnT-S assessment is designed to test foundational high-school level knowledge across four key scientific pillars and one logic pillar.

Mathematics (15 Questions)

  • Numbers & Sets: Prime numbers, GCD/LCM, fractions, decimals, percentages, absolute values, and powers/roots.
  • Algebra: Polynomial factorization, algebraic fractions, linear/quadratic equations, systems of equations, and inequalities.
  • Functions: Domain/range, inverse functions, composite functions, exponential and logarithmic functions, and basic trigonometry.
  • Geometry: Plane geometry (polygons, circles), solid geometry (volumes/surface area), and Cartesian coordinates.

Reasoning on Texts and Data (15 Questions)

  • Logical Reasoning: Propositions, logical connectives (AND, OR, NOT), sets, and deductive reasoning.
  • Data Analysis: Interpretation of complex tables, bar charts, line graphs, and pie charts.
  • Statistical Measures: Mean, median, mode, variance, and standard deviation calculations.

Biology (10 Questions)

  • Molecular Biology: DNA, RNA, protein synthesis, and genetic code.
  • Cell Biology: Structure of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, organelles, and cell membrane transport.
  • Genetics: Mendelian laws, inheritance patterns, and mutations.
  • Bioenergetics: ATP, cellular respiration, and photosynthesis basics.

Chemistry (10 Questions)

  • Matter Structure: Atomic model, isotopes, electron configuration, and the periodic table.
  • Chemical Bonds: Covalent, ionic, and metallic bonds, molecular geometry, and intermolecular forces.
  • Stoichiometry: Moles, chemical formulas, and balancing chemical reactions.
  • Acids & Bases: pH scale, strong/weak acids, and neutralization reactions.

Physics (5 Questions)

  • Kinematics & Dynamics: Uniform motion, acceleration, Newton’s laws, and friction.
  • Work & Energy: Kinetic energy, potential energy, and the principle of conservation of energy.
  • Electricity: Coulomb's law, electric field, Ohm's law, and simple circuits.

04. Scoring Logic & Normalization

This is where a lot of students trip up, so pay attention. CEnT-S uses a penalty-based scoring system followed by a statistical normalization process.

Raw Score Breakdown

  • Correct answer: +1.0 Point
  • Left blank: 0 Points
  • Wrong answer: -0.25 Point

Maximum Raw Score: 55.0
Minimum Raw Score: −13.75

Why Normalization Exists

Because multiple versions of the test are administered across different dates, CISIA applies a mathematical formula to "normalize" the scores. This ensures that a candidate who takes a "hard" version of the test is not penalized compared to someone who takes an "easy" version.

The official ranking score is typically the Normalized Score, which is calculated at the end of each macro-period. This score is what universities use for their merit rankings.

Quick tip: Due to the -0.25 penalty, it is statistically better to skip a question if you cannot eliminate at least two incorrect options. Blind guessing will likely hurt your final ranking.

05. Registration & Test Logistics

Registration is managed exclusively through the CISIA Student Portal. Here is the step-by-step:

  1. Create an Account: Register on the CISIA Portal using your passport or ID.
  2. Select the Test: Choose "CEnT-S" from the list of available exams.
  3. Choose Your Mode:
    • CEnT-S@HOME: Taken online with remote proctoring.
    • CEnT-S@UNI: Taken in-person at a university lab.
  4. Payment: Pay the administrative fee (typically €30.00). This fee is non-refundable.
  5. Test Certificate: After the test, you will receive a raw score certificate. The official normalized certificate is released at the end of the macro-period.

07. How to Prepare (A Realistic Plan)

Doing well on the CEnT-S requires a mix of solid science fundamentals and the ability to work under time pressure. Here is a 12-week study plan that actually works:

  • Weeks 1–4: Build the foundation. Focus on Mathematics and Reasoning first. These two sections make up 30 of the 55 questions, so getting comfortable here gives you the biggest score boost.
  • Weeks 5–8: Tackle the sciences. Dive into Chemistry and Biology. Physics only has 5 questions, so save it for last and do not let it eat your study time.
  • Weeks 9–12: Full simulations. Take at least 10 timed, full-length mock exams. Use the ItaloStudy CEnT-S Simulator to practice the section-by-section timing under realistic conditions.

08. Frequently Asked Questions

01. Is CEnT-S the same as TOLC-I?

Not exactly. While CEnT-S (CISIA English Test – Science) is based on the legacy TOLC-I structure, it is the new centralized version specifically designed for English-taught degrees. The timing (110 minutes) and question count (55) are the current standards for the 2026 intake.

02. How many times can I take the CEnT-S?

You can take the test once per macro-period. There are four macro-periods in the 2026 cycle (Nov-Jan, Feb-Mar, Apr-Jun, Sep-Oct). This gives you a maximum of four attempts per academic year, though most students aim for the first two sessions.

03. How does the normalized score work?

The normalized score is a statistical correction. If your version of the test was statistically harder than the average (determined by the performance of all candidates), your score is bumped up. This ensures fairness across different test dates.

04. Which universities accept the CEnT-S?

Most major Italian public universities with English STEM programs accept CEnT-S, including Politecnico di Milano, University of Bologna, University of Rome Sapienza, and University of Padua. Always check the specific "Bando" (Call for Admissions) for your target program.

05. Can I take CEnT-S from home?

Yes, CISIA offers the "CEnT-S@HOME" format. You will need a stable internet connection, a quiet room, and a specific proctoring software. Some universities, however, may still require the "CEnT-S@UNI" (in-person) format for specific enrollment windows.

06. Is there a penalty for wrong answers?

Yes. Correct answers earn 1 point, while incorrect answers deduct 0.25 points. Unanswered questions result in 0 points. Statistically, it is better to leave a question blank than to make a completely random guess.

07. Does CEnT-S replace the English language certificate?

Usually, no. CEnT-S tests your scientific knowledge in English, but most universities still require a separate B2-level certificate (like IELTS 6.0+ or TOEFL iBT) for final enrollment. Check if your university offers an internal English test.

08. What is a "competitive" score for CEnT-S?

For top-tier Engineering programs (e.g., Computer Science at Sapienza), a normalized score above 35-40 is typically competitive. For less crowded programs, scores around 25-30 may be sufficient.

09. Can I go back to a previous section during the test?

No. Once the timer for a section (e.g., Mathematics) expires or you click "Next Section," you cannot return to those questions. You must manage your time strictly within each block.

10. How long is the CEnT-S score valid?

The CEnT-S score is valid for the academic year of the test. However, some universities may accept scores from the previous calendar year. Always verify the "validity period" section in the official university admission call.

11. What materials am I allowed to use?

Calculators, books, and mobile phones are strictly prohibited. You are typically allowed a few blank sheets of paper and a pen for rough work, which must be shown to the proctor if taking the test at home.
