IMAT 2026: Your Complete Guide to Studying Medicine in Italy

Everything you need to know about the International Medical Admissions Test — from how the exam works to how the national ranking decides where you study. Written by the ItaloStudy editorial team.

Last Updated May 2026
Reading Time 15 Minutes
Status Verified & Up to Date

01. What is the IMAT?

The International Medical Admissions Test (IMAT) is the single entrance exam for English-taught Medicine and Surgery (and Dentistry) programs at public universities across Italy. It is administered under the authority of the Ministry of University and Research (MUR) and takes place once a year, usually in mid-September.

Here is the key thing that catches most applicants off guard: the IMAT is not an exam you pass or fail. It is a national competition. Every candidate in the country (and from abroad) receives a score, and those scores are placed into one massive ranking list. Your position on that list determines which university you get into — or whether you get in at all.

Important: Your IMAT score is only valid for the academic year you take the test. You cannot carry over a score from a previous year.

02. How the Test Works

The IMAT is a paper-based exam with 60 multiple-choice questions. Each question gives you five answer options, and only one is correct. You have 100 minutes to complete the entire test — no breaks, no sections.

How Scoring Works

  • Correct answer: +1.5 points
  • Wrong answer: −0.4 points (yes, there is a penalty)
  • Left blank: 0 points

That gives you a maximum possible score of 90.0. EU candidates need at least 20.0 to appear in the ranking. Non-EU candidates do not have an official minimum, but in practice, you will need much higher to compete for the limited Non-EU seats.

03. 2026 Official Syllabus Breakdown

The syllabus is established by the Italian secondary school curriculum standards but is aligned with international A-Level and IB standards.

Biology (23 Questions - High Yield)

Biology is the most critical section. Candidates are assessed on:

  • Chemistry of Life: Bioelements, water properties, and organic molecules (lipids, proteins, carbohydrates, nucleic acids).
  • The Cell: Theory, prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic, organelles, cell cycle, mitosis, and meiosis.
  • Genetics: Mendelian genetics, DNA/RNA structure, transcription, translation, and human genetic diseases.
  • Anatomy & Physiology: Respiratory, circulatory, digestive, nervous, and endocrine systems in humans.

Chemistry (15 Questions)

  • Atomic Structure: Electron configuration, periodic table trends, and chemical bonding.
  • Stoichiometry: Moles, balancing equations, and gas laws.
  • Equilibria: Acid-base chemistry, pH calculations, and redox reactions.
  • Organic Chemistry: Isomerism, functional groups, and basic reaction mechanisms.

Physics & Mathematics (13 Questions)

  • Physics: Kinematics, dynamics, thermodynamics, electricity, and optics.
  • Mathematics: Algebra, geometry, trigonometry, and basic probability.
Heads up: Since 2024, Logical Reasoning was cut down to just 5 questions. The exam now leans heavily toward technical science, so Biology and Chemistry should get most of your study time.

04. The National Merit Ranking & Scrolled Allocation

This is the most complex part of the IMAT system. Italy uses a Graduatoria Nazionale (National Ranking) for EU students and separate local rankings for Non-EU students.

EU vs Non-EU Status

  • EU Candidates: Citizens of the European Union, or Non-EU citizens holding a long-term residence permit in Italy. They compete for a shared pool of seats across all universities.
  • Non-EU Candidates: Citizens residing outside the EU. They compete only for the specific seats allocated to Non-EU students at their first-choice university.

The "Scrolling" (Scorrimenti) System

For EU candidates, the ranking "scrolls" every week. If a candidate ranked #100 decides not to enroll at Milan, their seat is offered to candidate #101. This process continues for several months until all seats are filled.

Status types in the ranking:

  • Assegnato (Assigned): You got your first choice. You MUST enroll within 4 days or you lose your seat.
  • Prenotato (Booked): You got a lower choice. You can enroll now or wait for a higher choice in the next scroll.
  • In Attesa (Waiting): All your choices are currently full. You must wait for more scrolls.

06. Registration & Application Steps

For 2026, the application follows a strict sequence. Miss one step and you are out.

  1. Universitaly Registration: Typically in July. You must list your university choices in order of preference.
  2. Payment of Test Fee: Paid via the Universitaly portal (approx. €130-150).
  3. The Exam: September. You must bring your passport and registration receipt.
  4. The Ranking: Results are published in late September/October.
Do not skip this: Once you submit your university preferences on Universitaly, they cannot be changed. Your first choice is the only place Non-EU students can be admitted.

07. How to Actually Prepare

Forget generic study advice. Here is what separates students who get in from students who do not:

  • Understand the penalty math. With +1.5 for correct and −0.4 for wrong, a random guess has negative expected value. Learn when to skip — it could be the difference between admission and a gap year.
  • Do every past paper. IMAT papers from 2011 to 2025 are all publicly available. The question style is very specific, and there is no substitute for practicing the real thing.
  • Get comfortable without a calculator. Calculators are banned. You need to be able to do mental arithmetic, unit conversions, and basic logs by hand without hesitating.
  • Go deep on Biology. Surface-level memorization is not enough. You need to understand how things work — membrane transport, gene expression, enzyme kinetics — because the questions test understanding, not recall.

08. Frequently Asked Questions

01. Is the IMAT paper-based or computer-based?

The IMAT is a paper-based exam. Candidates are provided with an optical answer sheet that is scanned by a machine. You must use a black pen and follow strict instructions on how to mark your answers.

02. Can I take the IMAT more than once per year?

No. Unlike the CEnT-S or TOLC exams, the IMAT is held only once a year, typically in mid-September. If you do not succeed, you must wait until the following year's cycle.

03. How many universities can I apply to?

You can list as many participating public universities as you want on your Universitaly application. However, you must list them in order of preference. This order is final and cannot be changed after the registration deadline.

04. What is the "Minimum Score" for admission?

For EU and equated candidates, the minimum threshold to be included in the ranking is 20 points. For Non-EU candidates, there is technically no minimum, but due to high competition, the actual admission scores are much higher.

05. What is the difference between an "Assigned" and "Booked" status?

An "Assigned" status means you got into your first choice. You must enroll within 4 days. A "Booked" status means you got into a lower choice. you can either enroll there now or wait for future "scrolling" to see if a seat opens in a higher preference.

06. Can I use a calculator during the exam?

No calculators, periodic tables, or reference sheets are allowed. You must be proficient in mental math and basic scientific constants (e.g., Avogadro's number, gravity, gas constant).

07. Where are the IMAT test centers located?

There are centers in all major Italian cities and several international locations including London, New York, Dubai, New Delhi, Sao Paulo, and more. The test is identical and held simultaneously worldwide.

08. Is the IMAT valid for private universities like Humanitas or Cattolica?

No. Private medical schools in Italy have their own separate entrance exams (e.g., HUMAT for Humanitas). The IMAT is exclusively for public universities.

09. How does the penalty scoring affect my strategy?

With a -0.4 penalty for wrong answers, guessing is risky. If you can eliminate 3 out of 5 options, the statistical expectation of a guess becomes positive. If you have no idea, it is better to leave it blank (0 points).

10. What is a "competitive" score for a top university?

For universities like Milan or Bologna, EU students often need 50-55+ points. Non-EU students may need 60+ points depending on the specific year's difficulty and the number of available seats.

11. Do I need an English certificate like IELTS?

While the IMAT is in English, some universities may still ask for an IELTS (6.0+) or TOEFL certificate during the final enrollment phase. However, many consider the IMAT itself as proof of English proficiency. Check your first-choice's "Bando."

12. How much does it cost to study medicine in Italy?

Tuition fees at public universities are based on family income (ISEE). They typically range from €156 to €4,000 per year. Many international students qualify for DSU scholarships which can reduce tuition to nearly zero.
