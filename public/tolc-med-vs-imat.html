TOLC-Med vs IMAT (CEnT-S) 2027: Which Exam Do You Need?

By ItaloStudy Editorial Team — Italy admissions & entrance exam specialists  |  Last updated: November 2026  |  About us

Confused about Italy's medical entrance exams? This guide clarifies the difference between TOLC-Med and IMAT/CEnT-S, who takes which, and what the transition means for you.

The Short Answer: Two Different Tracks

Italy has two separate tracks for studying medicine:

  • TOLC-Med: For Italian-taught medical programs — primarily taken by Italian and EU students
  • IMAT/CEnT-S: For English-taught medical programs — primarily taken by international students

These are completely separate exams for separate programs. You don't choose between them based on difficulty — you choose based on which language you want to study medicine in.

💡 The IMAT → CEnT-S Transition

IMAT (International Medical Admissions Test) is being replaced by CEnT-S (Centro di Eccellenza Test — Scienze) as the admission test for English-taught medical programs. This is NOT the same as TOLC-Med. CEnT-S is the direct successor to IMAT for English programs. If you're an international student wanting to study in English, CEnT-S is your exam.

The Evolution of Italy's Medical Entrance Exams

Timeline of Changes

2007
Italy introduces English-taught medical programs at select universities, using their own admission tests.
2011
IMAT launched — a centralized, standardized English-language admission test created by Cambridge Assessment, replacing individual university tests.
2013
TOLC (Test OnLine CISIA) system introduced for various Italian university programs, including engineering and sciences.
2023
TOLC-Med introduced as the standardized admission test for Italian-taught medical programs, replacing the previous national test.
2024-25
Transition period: IMAT managed by CISIA instead of Cambridge Assessment. CEnT-S announced as the new admission format for English programs.
2026-27
CEnT-S expected to fully replace IMAT for English-taught program admissions. Both TOLC-Med and CEnT-S administered by CISIA.

Difficulty Comparison

Science Question Difficulty

TOLC-Med science questions align with the Italian high school curriculum (liceo scientifico), which goes deeper in some areas than what IMAT tests. Italian students preparing for TOLC-Med study physics at a higher mathematical level and have more detailed organic chemistry requirements.

IMAT/CEnT-S science questions are calibrated for an international audience — they test broad understanding rather than curriculum-specific depth.

Non-Science Sections

This is where the exams differ most:

  • TOLC-Med: Includes reading comprehension in Italian — tests language ability and scientific text interpretation
  • IMAT/CEnT-S: Includes logical reasoning and general knowledge — tests critical thinking, argument analysis, and broad cultural knowledge

Overall Assessment

Neither exam is definitively "harder" — they test different skill sets. An Italian student who grew up in the Italian school system finds TOLC-Med more natural. An international student with strong English and critical thinking skills finds IMAT/CEnT-S more suitable.

🎯 Strategic Tip for Bilingual Students: If you're fluent in both Italian and English, you have a unique advantage. You can take TOLC-Med (with its 60,000+ seats) AND CEnT-S (for English programs) in the same year, maximizing your chances. The exam dates don't overlap. Some competitive students use this dual approach to ensure they get a seat somewhere.

What the IMAT → CEnT-S Transition Means for You

What's Changing

  • Name: IMAT → CEnT-S (Centro di Eccellenza Test — Scienze)
  • Administrator: Cambridge Assessment → CISIA (same organization that runs TOLC-Med)
  • Format: May be adjusted — check official announcements for the latest structure
  • Possibly multiple sittings: CISIA may allow multiple attempts (like TOLC-Med), though this hasn't been confirmed

What's NOT Changing

  • Language: Still in English
  • Purpose: Still for English-taught medical programs in Italy
  • Core subjects: Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Math remain
  • Target audience: International students and EU students preferring English
  • Universities: Same universities that offered IMAT will accept CEnT-S
⚠️ Stay Updated: The transition from IMAT to CEnT-S is ongoing, and details may change. Always check the official CISIA website and your target university's admissions page for the most current information. ItaloStudy updates its preparation materials to match the latest exam format.

Summary: Which Exam is Right for You?

  • Take CEnT-S (successor to IMAT) if you want English-taught medicine, are an international student, or prefer studying in English
  • Take TOLC-Med if you're fluent in Italian and want access to the widest range of Italian medical schools with the most seats
  • Take both if you're bilingual — double your chances by applying to both Italian and English programs

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between TOLC-Med and IMAT?
TOLC-Med is for Italian-taught medical programs (primarily Italian/EU students). IMAT/CEnT-S is for English-taught programs (primarily international students). They're separate exams for separate programs — you choose based on the language of instruction, not difficulty.
Has IMAT been replaced by TOLC-Med?
No. IMAT is being replaced by CEnT-S, not TOLC-Med. TOLC-Med is a completely different track for Italian-language programs. CEnT-S is the direct successor to IMAT for English-taught programs. Both are now administered by CISIA.
Can international students take TOLC-Med?
Technically yes, if you're fluent in Italian. But most international students take IMAT/CEnT-S instead, since it's in English and designed for international applicants. TOLC-Med offers more seats (60,000+ vs 1,800) but requires Italian language proficiency.
Is TOLC-Med harder than IMAT?
TOLC-Med has deeper science content aligned with Italian high school curriculum. IMAT has unique logical reasoning and general knowledge sections. Neither is definitively harder — it depends on your background. Italian students find TOLC-Med more natural; international students find IMAT more suitable.
Can I take both TOLC-Med and CEnT-S?
Yes, if you're fluent in both Italian and English. The exam dates don't overlap, so bilingual students can take both to maximize their chances of securing a medical seat in Italy.
