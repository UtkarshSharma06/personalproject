IMAT Past Paper Analysis by Subject

Understanding the subject-wise breakdown of IMAT past papers helps you prioritize your preparation effectively.

Section 1: General Knowledge & Logical Reasoning (22 Questions)

This section tests critical thinking, problem-solving, and general knowledge. It includes 10 logical reasoning questions and 12 general knowledge questions. The logical reasoning questions have remained consistent in difficulty across years, while general knowledge topics vary significantly.

  • Logical Reasoning (10 Qs): Pattern recognition, syllogisms, critical analysis of arguments, data interpretation
  • General Knowledge (12 Qs): History, literature, economics, politics, geography — topics change every year

Section 2: Biology (18 Questions)

Biology carries the highest weightage among science subjects. Past paper analysis shows these topics appear most frequently:

  • Cell Biology & Genetics: Appears in 90% of past papers — DNA replication, cell division, Mendelian genetics
  • Human Anatomy & Physiology: Cardiovascular, nervous, and endocrine systems are recurring favorites
  • Molecular Biology: Protein synthesis, enzyme kinetics, metabolic pathways
  • Ecology & Evolution: 2-3 questions per year on average

Section 3: Chemistry (12 Questions)

Chemistry questions in IMAT focus heavily on:

  • Organic Chemistry: 4-5 questions per paper — functional groups, reaction mechanisms, nomenclature
  • Physical Chemistry: 3-4 questions — thermodynamics, equilibrium, stoichiometry
  • Inorganic Chemistry: 2-3 questions — periodic table trends, bonding, transition metals

Section 4: Physics & Mathematics (8 Questions)

The smallest section but often the most scoring for well-prepared students:

  • Physics (5 Qs): Mechanics, electricity, optics, thermodynamics
  • Mathematics (3 Qs): Probability, algebra, geometry — straightforward if you know the formulas

How to Use IMAT Past Papers Effectively

Simply reading through past papers won't help. Here's the proven 4-step method used by top scorers:

Step 1: Diagnostic Test (Week 1)

Take the most recent IMAT paper (2026) under timed conditions without any preparation. This gives you a baseline score and reveals your strong and weak subjects. Don't worry if you score low — most students score 20-30 on their first attempt.

Step 2: Subject-Wise Practice (Weeks 2-8)

After identifying weak areas, study those subjects using ItaloStudy's question bank. Then attempt the subject-specific questions from past papers (2020-2025) to check your understanding. Focus on one subject per week.

Step 3: Full Paper Practice (Weeks 9-12)

Take 2 full past papers per week under strict exam conditions. After each paper, spend equal time reviewing your answers — especially the ones you got right by guessing. Use the IMAT scoring system (-0.4 for wrong answers) to calculate your real score.

Step 4: Final Revision (Last 2 Weeks)

Re-attempt your weakest papers. Focus on papers from 2022-2026 as they best represent current difficulty levels. Your target: consistently scoring above 40/90 (the typical cutoff range).

⏰ Pro Tip: Take each past paper at the same time your actual IMAT exam will be held (typically morning). This trains your brain to peak at the right time. Studies show this can improve performance by 5-8%.

IMAT Past Paper Score Trends (2016-2026)

Understanding score trends helps you set realistic targets:

  • Average cutoff for top universities (Sapienza, Bologna): 40-50 points out of 90
  • Average cutoff for mid-tier universities: 30-40 points
  • Minimum passing score (varies by year): 20-25 points
  • Highest recorded scores: 75-82 points (top 0.1% of candidates)

The exam has gotten progressively harder since 2022 when the IMAT transitioned to CISIA management. Students preparing for IMAT 2027 should focus on 2022-2026 papers for the most accurate difficulty representation.

Common Mistakes Students Make with Past Papers

  1. Starting too late: Begin past paper practice at least 3 months before the exam, not 2 weeks before
  2. Not timing themselves: Practicing without a timer gives false confidence. Always use the 100-minute limit.
  3. Skipping the review: The learning happens in the review, not during the test. Spend 2x the test time reviewing.
  4. Ignoring negative marking: IMAT deducts 0.4 points per wrong answer. Learn when to skip vs. guess.
  5. Only doing recent papers: Older papers (2016-2019) are easier and great for building confidence early in preparation.
